Hi everyone, welcome. Thank you for joining me. Today, I am excited to introduce PaperQuiz, an AI-supported tool that helps students turn course materials into active practice sets.

Before entering the Dashboard, a new user starts on the Sign in page. The user can choose Password, Email link, or Continue with Google.

The Email link option sends a secure sign-in link to the user’s inbox. After signing in, the user can sync practice history and the Mistake Book across devices.

This is the Dashboard, where students can see what to study today. 

To create a new set, I click Choose a PDF or lecture recording. PaperQuiz supports PDFs and lecture recordings, and students can also drag a file into the upload area, like this.

Next, students choose the question mix. They can adjust the number of Multiple-choice questions, Fill-blank questions, and Short-answer questions. They can also write an optional instruction under Anything specific for this set? For example, they can ask PaperQuiz to focus on a specific chapter or use simpler language. When everything is ready, they click Generate quiz.

Here is a prepared question. Depending on the question type, I can choose an option or type an answer, and then click Submit answer. PaperQuiz shows feedback and a detailed explanation. If I want to know more, I can use Ask to request a focused explanation or additional help about this question. If I am not ready to answer, I can click Skip to continue.

Recent-practice cards let students reopen saved practice sets, and shortcut cards take them directly to the main study areas.

When I finish the quiz, I can click Exit this quiz to return to the dashboard. On the results page, I can download a Student copy with no answers or an Answer key with answers. I can also open the Mistake Book.

The Mistake Book is one of the most important parts of PaperQuiz. Wrong answers are automatically saved here for targeted review. Students can click Practice all to review them again, filter them by question type, or select individual questions for a focused practice session. View details shows the question, my answer, the correct answer, and the explanation. Practice again lets me retry one question, and Export PDF creates a portable review copy for offline practice.

Next is the Calendar. Students can track their practice and see the next review schedule. I can click Previous and Next to browse months, click a date to see study activity, and use Start review to practice questions that are due that day. The Accuracy trend gives students a clear picture of their overall progress.

The review schedule is based on the Ebbinghaus forgetting curve. I can click How is this schedule decided? to expand the explanation. It shows that a wrong question joins a review ladder. The intervals become wider over time: one, two, four, seven, fifteen, and thirty days. If a student answers correctly, the question moves to the next step. If the student answers incorrectly, it returns to the first step and appears again the next day. This helps students review just before they are likely to forget, instead of studying everything on the same day.

Students can also view their Library. It is organized by courses and PDFs. Students can click Sort materials, switch between Grid view and List view, assign a PDF to a course, create a new course, and use Open to return to a material’s practice context.

Inside a generated review sheet, students can click a source-page image to enlarge it and read the original PDF page more clearly. They can click Open PDF at this page to open the original document at the cited page, and click Close to return to the review sheet.

Finally, PaperQuiz includes a product-help Chatbot. Users can ask questions such as, “How do I upload a lecture?” and it will explain which PaperQuiz buttons to use.

Users can click Feedback to report a bug or suggest an improvement. This opens my Google Form.

Here are Dark mode and Light mode, which change the appearance of the website.

Students can also use the language button to switch the interface language.

There is also a Settings panel, where users can see account and usage information. The credit buttons are available when billing is needed, but there is no need to use them in this demonstration.

In conclusion, PaperQuiz is not only a quiz generator. It connects materials, practice, feedback, mistakes, review scheduling, and progress in one learning workflow. Thank you for listening.
